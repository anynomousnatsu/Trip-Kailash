# Trip Kailash Design Package

The single creative deliverable. Every decision for the redesign lives here, and every
build phase consumes it. Whoever picks this up should need nothing else to build the right
page.

Two rules govern this document:

- **Every line of copy here ships verbatim.** This is where the writing happens; the
  templates are where the wiring happens. Build passes wire these lines in exactly and
  never paraphrase them.
- **Numbers are starting points.** Band ranges and pacing numbers are labelled as starting
  points. The flick test validates them against the real footage, and the ranges move if
  the test says so.

Status: Phase 0 deliverable. Written 23 August 2026, revised the same day after customer
research.

---

## 0. The research

Done before the design, per the method: find the exact language buyers use about their
pains, desires and hesitations, then write the site in those words. Sources are listed at
the end of this section.

### 0.1 The finding that changes the strategy

**2026 is the Tibetan Fire Horse Year.** A Horse Year comes every twelve; a *Fire* Horse
Year comes every sixty. Across Shaiva, Buddhist, Jain and Bön traditions it is held to be
the most auspicious time in a lifetime to complete the Kailash kora, and one circuit walked
in a Horse Year is said to carry the merit of thirteen ordinary ones. The last was 2026.
The next Horse Year is 2038. The next Fire Horse Year is 2086.

Two facts sit on top of that:

- **The 2026 season closes around 30 September.** Operators list final departures between
  30 September and 3 October. Today is 23 August 2026, so roughly five weeks remain.
- **tripkailash.com says nothing about any of it.** A company whose name is Kailash, whose
  flagship is the kora, went through the most significant Kailash year in six decades
  without the website mentioning it once.

This produces two positions, both true, and the site needs both:

**The urgent one, only if a September departure can actually run.** A time-boxed band at
the top of the Kailash material. It comes down on 1 October and is never faked.

**The durable one, and it is the better piece of positioning.** Every competitor is selling
the hype year. Almost nobody will say the honest, useful thing afterwards:

> The Fire Horse Year is over. Tens of thousands walked the kora in 2026 and the trail
> showed it. 2027 is quiet. Permits come easier, lodges are not full, and your guide is
> looking after eight people instead of thirty. If you want the mountain rather than the
> crowd, this is the better year to walk it.

That reframes the brief's weakest asset. Kailash had no auspicious-year hook for 2027;
now it has a genuine advantage that competing operators cannot copy without contradicting
their own 2026 marketing.

### 0.2 The pains, in buyers' own words

| Pain | What the research actually says |
|---|---|
| **Fear of being defrauded** | This is the number one objection, and it is not paranoia. A Hyderabad operator took over ₹15 lakh from seven Bengaluru pilgrims and never ran the trip. Sponsored emails circulate faking operator credentials. One widely read piece is titled, plainly, that the yatra is a long scam. |
| **Money vanishing into permits** | Deposits of 20 to 30% are standard, and are commonly non-refundable. Once a Tibet permit is applied for, no refund is available even if the trip cannot proceed. Buyers know this and are braced for vagueness about it. |
| **Altitude, badly handled** | About 35% fall ill if flown straight to Mansarovar at 4,550 m. Pilgrims who skip acclimatisation days find Dolma La at 5,630 m extremely difficult, and some are turned back before completing the kora. |
| **Preparing the wrong thing** | The sharpest line in the whole research: pilgrims spend months preparing the spiritual side and treat altitude as an afterthought, which is exactly why it catches them out. |
| **Booking for elderly parents** | A large share of buyers are adult children booking for a parent. Their language is about duty and dignity, not adventure. They fear the road journey more than the shrine. |
| **Lead time** | 3 to 6 months is normal, 4 to 6 for a Horse Year. Anyone arriving late has already been told no elsewhere. |

### 0.3 The desire, in buyers' own words

Not scenery, and not achievement. The recurring words are *burn away*, *inner journey*,
*felt the presence*, *transformation*, *sins*. Pilgrims say the kora is a journey inward
that happens to take place on a mountain. They also, consistently, want to tell people
afterwards.

Two consequences for the site:

1. **Never sell Kailash as a trek.** Every competitor does, and it is the wrong frame for
   the person actually buying.
2. **Build the testimonial pipeline into the product.** Pilgrims want to share. Ask at the
   right moment and the site's biggest current weakness, no reviews, closes itself.

### 0.4 The objection that hands us the trust strategy

From guidance written for buyers on vetting Nepali operators:

> An agency that is properly registered will actively encourage you to verify their
> credentials rather than asking you to take their word for it. If a company cannot point
> you at its Nepal Tourism Board registration number when asked, nothing else it tells you
> is worth evaluating.

Both registers are public. TAAN membership is searchable at `taan.org.np`; NTB
registration is confirmable through the Board. **So the site should not display credential
numbers, it should invite verification of them, with the links.** No competitor does this.
It costs nothing, it is impossible to fake, and it answers the number one objection in the
category directly. This becomes a named section, not a footer line.

### 0.5 The one call to action

Everything on the site funnels to **one enquiry**, which is a conversation and not a
purchase. That matches how the business actually closes: enquiry, confirmation, invoice,
30% deposit. The site never pretends to be a checkout.

## 0.5 Tier and the Creative Director's Loop

### The tier: Tier 1, the single journey

One 6-second shot scrubbed by scroll, captions in the negative space, the page settling at
the composed ending. Chosen because the footage already exists or will be a single clip,
because a first build starts here, and because the boldness budget is being spent on the
pinned parikrama gallery and the kora ring rather than on a chained hero. Tier 2 chaining
is a later upgrade and costs nothing to move to.

### The loop, all seven hats

**Producer.** No generation credits are planned. Assets are the operator's own footage and
photography, so cost is time rather than credits. The mobile decision is made and stated:
phones get the designed static hero, because most of this traffic is Indian and Nepali
mobile and the video is never worth its weight there. If no usable clip exists, the hero
becomes a composed still and the pinned gallery carries the signature alone; that swap
costs one section and no rework elsewhere.

**Researcher.** Section 0 above. One call to action: the enquiry.

**Storyboarder.** One chapter, four beats: the cold wide, the trail with the flags, the
pass, the settle. The exact frame the page rests on is the composed ending, which is also
harvested as a design asset for the lower sections.

**Prompt generator.** Not applicable at Tier 1 with supplied footage. If a shot is ever
generated, the twelve laws in §7.3 govern it.

**Designer.** Palette §2, type §3, motif system and vector layer §7, text effects synced
to the beats in §4.

**Website producer.** Chapter captions §4, the settle, the sections in §6, the one
interactive moment in §6.4, and the conversion furniture funnelling to the single enquiry.

**Gatekeeper.** Gates in order: this package approved, then the footage validated against
the laws in §7.3, then the build self-tested against the checklist, then the copy gate,
then live verification with speed receipts.

## 1. The brand premise

> **Parikrama.** You do not climb what is sacred. You walk around it. Kailash has never
> been summited and never will be. Pilgrims walk a 52 kilometre circle around its base and
> come down changed. Every journey Trip Kailash runs is a circle around something: a lake,
> a cave, a flame, a mountain. Trekking companies sell the top. We sell the circle.

Every section on the site teaches and sells that one idea. The catalogue is organised as
circles. The signature interaction is a circle the visitor walks. Kailash sits last in
every sequence because you arrive at the mountain, you do not start there.

If a section does not serve the premise, it does not belong on the page.

**Why this premise, confirmed by the research.** The circle is not decoration, it is the
unit of merit. A Horse Year multiplies koras, not summits. Pilgrims describe the kora as a
journey inward that burns away what they carry. Every competitor sells Muktinath as a trek
and Kailash as a tour, which is the wrong frame for the person actually buying. And it
solves the commercial problem: framed as the circle no one climbs, Kailash becomes the
thing the catalogue is measured against rather than a $2,500 ask from a stranger.

## 2. The palette as CSS tokens

Three families carry the whole site. Sandalwood carries surfaces, brass carries structure
and ornament, vermilion is reserved for one job. Bark is the ink. One night ground is added
for the cinematic sections.

```css
:root{
  /* Day ground. The page lives here. */
  --sand-50:#FBF6EC;   /* page ground */
  --sand-100:#F5EDDF;  /* raised */
  --sand-200:#EBDFC9;  /* inset */
  --sand-300:#DCCAA9;  /* dividers */

  /* Night ground. The cinematic canvas: hero, gallery, footer. Warm, never #000. */
  --night-900:#0E0904;
  --night-800:#1E1006;
  --night-700:#33200D;

  /* Brass. Structure and ornament. */
  --brass-300:#E0C489;  /* ornament on dark */
  --brass-400:#C9A24C;  /* icons on light */
  --brass-500:#B8860B;  /* rules, focus ring */
  --brass-600:#8A6408;  /* brass text on sand, passes AA */

  /* Vermilion. The booking action and nothing else. */
  --verm-400:#C8593A;  /* hover */
  --verm-500:#A8452C;  /* THE booking action */
  --verm-600:#86341F;  /* pressed */

  /* Bark. The ink. */
  --bark-900:#1E1006;  /* headings */
  --bark-800:#33200D;  /* body */
  --bark-500:#75603F;  /* muted, meta */
  --bark-300:#A9906B;  /* placeholder only, never body */

  /* Grading. Colour always paired with the word, never colour alone. */
  --grade-easy:#5C7A3F; --grade-moderate:#B8860B; --grade-hard:#A8452C;

  /* Hairlines. Depth comes from these plus sand steps, never from shadows. */
  --hair:rgba(51,32,13,.13);
  --solid:rgba(51,32,13,.24);
  --brassline:rgba(184,134,11,.42);
}
```

**Rules that hold everywhere.**

1. **Vermilion is rationed.** It appears on booking actions and nowhere else. Not headings,
   not icons, not link hovers. Sindoor is a mark applied to one spot, not a wash. If it is
   on eleven things per page it stops meaning "act here".
2. **No drop shadows.** Depth is three steps of sandalwood plus hairline borders. Shadows on
   a warm palette turn muddy and are the visual tell of a page-builder site.
3. **Small radii.** Temple architecture is cut stone, not soft plastic. `--r:4px` for most
   things, `--rlg:10px` for cards only. The one curve in the system is the torana arch, and
   it is drawn, not rounded.
4. **The canvas is never pure black or pure white.** Sand at the top of the ramp, warm
   near-black at the bottom.
5. **Sandalwood is also the practical choice.** Photography is currently a mix with gaps,
   and muted warm backgrounds hide inconsistent white balance where a bright white page
   exposes every bad frame.

## 3. The type trio

| Role | Face | Weights in use |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Cinzel** | 700, 900 |
| Body | **Karla** | 400 for long prose, 500 default |
| Devanagari | **Noto Serif Devanagari** | 500, 700 |

Cinzel is Trajan-column inscriptional lettering, carved rather than printed, which is where
the monumental feeling comes from. It renders essentially as capitals, so it is slower to
read: restrict it to headings, buttons and small labels. Body copy stays in Karla. If the
hero reads underweight at full size, fix it with size and tracking, not weight. Cinzel is
already at 900.

Devanagari is a first-class font here, not a fallback. Packages use terms like
*Daśa-Divasīya Śiva-Parikramā*. Rendering those in a system fallback reads as carelessness
about the tradition.

**Deviation from the 10k standard, stated out loud.** The method asks for a mono for small
labels. This brand's small-label voice is Cinzel at 12px with 0.22em tracking, and it is
better here than a mono would be. A mono reads techy against temple stone. Numeric readouts
(5,630 m · 52 km · 14 days) get `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on Karla instead.
Three faces, not four, which also matters on the connections this audience is on.

**Self-host all three** in `assets/fonts/`, trimmed to exactly the weights above, `woff2`
only, `font-display: swap`. No request to `fonts.googleapis.com`. The DNS lookup and round
trip cost real seconds on Indian and Nepali mobile.

| Band | Range | Footage moment | Copy (verbatim) | Entrance |

which was pleasant and said nothing. The merit multiplier is the single fact that reframes
the entire decision for a buyer in this category, and it belongs at the moment the footage
reaches the pass.

**Verify before shipping band 3.** Sources cite the Horse Year multiplier as thirteen, some
as twelve. Confirm the figure your own tradition uses. Getting religious detail wrong on a
pilgrimage site is worse than saying nothing.

**Band 4 copy, verbatim.**

Headline (already approved, final):
> Walk the sacred paths of Nepal with people who know the rituals

Subline (em dash removed so it passes the copy gate):
> Muktinath, Gosaikunda, Haleshi and Kailash, led by guides who understand what you have
> come to do, not only how to get you there.

Actions:
- Primary, vermilion: **Find your yatra** → `#paths`
- Ghost: **Kailash, and which year to walk it** → `#departures`

The second action changed too. "Kailash 2027" was a date; this is the question the
high-intent visitor is actually carrying, and it leads to the section that answers it.

Licence line, pinned below the fold edge:
> Licensed by the Government of Nepal. Reg. [NUMBER]. The pilgrimage arm of Trekmania Nepal.

**Legibility.** Every band gets all four layers: the global base scrim over the whole video,
a per-band radial scrim riding that band's `--k`, the three-layer text shadow token, and
chip backings on any small label over footage. Peak scrim alphas start at 0.62 for the
quiet beats and 0.72 for band 1, then get tuned against each band's own worst frame. Every
band must clear 3.5:1 worst-pixel contrast before anyone sees the page.

## 6. The below-fold outline

Nine sections. No two adjacent sections share a layout skeleton. Every section funnels
toward one call to action: the enquiry.

### 6.1 Hero
Covered above. Settles into the approved line and the two actions.

### 6.2 Choose your path
Day ground. Three doors in a row, each with its own drawn geometry.

Sits **above** the packages deliberately. It reframes what the visitor thinks they are
shopping for before they see a price. Once someone is comparing $350 to $1,000, the
pilgrimage framing is gone and you are competing on cost with all of Thamel.

- Eyebrow: `CHOOSE YOUR PATH`
- Heading: **Which tradition are you travelling in?**

| Door | Devanagari | Body copy (verbatim) | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shaiva | शैव | The abodes of Shiva. Kailash, Gosaikunda, Kedarnath, and the temple parikrama of the valley. | 5 yatras |
| Buddhist | बौद्ध | Maratika, the Chumig Gyatsa at Muktinath, and the kora of Kailash. Where Padmasambhava walked. | 3 yatras |
| Vaishnava | वैष्णव | Muktinath. Mukti Kshetra, the place of liberation, and the shaligram beds of the Kali Gandaki. | 1 yatra |

Counts are computed live from the Tradition taxonomy, never typed. Several sites belong to
more than one faith, which is why Tradition is multi-select and why the counts do not sum
to the package total.

**Geometry, one per door, never one icon recoloured:** torana arch (Shaiva), dharma wheel
(Buddhist), shaligram facet (Vaishnava). Each draws itself on scroll.

Do not apologise for the Shaiva weighting. "Nepal's Shiva pilgrimage specialists" is
sharper positioning than pretending to cover everything.

### 6.3 The parikrama
Night ground, pinned horizontal scroll. **The signature section.** It fades in and out
through the sand via top and bottom gradient masks so it never reads as a stripe.

- Eyebrow: `THE CIRCLES WE WALK`
- Heading: **Journeys we know by heart**

Order is deliberate and fixed: Muktinath → Gosaikunda → Maratika → Shiva Divine →
**Kailash last**. You arrive at the mountain.

Copy in this section describes **sacred meaning, never logistics**. Facts sit in the small
row underneath. Every competitor leads with logistics, which is exactly why this does not.

**Muktinath** · Vaishnava · Buddhist
> The place of liberation. One courtyard holds 108 spouts fed by the Kali Gandaki, and a
> flame that has burned on natural gas since before it was named. Water and fire in the
> same shrine.

Facts: 8 days · 3,710 m · from $1,000

**Gosaikunda** · Shaiva
> Struck from the rock by Shiva's trident after he swallowed the poison of the churning
> ocean. At Janai Purnima, thirty thousand people bathe here in a single night at 4,600
> metres to wash away what they carry.

Facts: 5 days · 4,600 m · from $350

**Haleshi Maratika** · Shaiva · Buddhist
> Shiva's hiding place from Bhasmasura to Hindus. To Buddhists, the cave where
> Padmasambhava and Mandarava won deathlessness. One of the great Nyingma sites, and
> almost unvisited.

Facts: 5 days · 1,443 m · from $500

**Shiva Divine Yatra** · Shaiva
> Ten days of parikrama across the Shiva temples of Nepal, each arrival timed to its
> evening ritual rather than to a driving schedule. Pashupatinath, Doleshwor, Gokarneshwor
> and beyond.

Facts: 10 days · 3,710 m · from $1,000

**Kailash Mansarovar** · Shaiva · Buddhist · Jain · Bön
> The mountain no one climbs. Four faiths, four rivers, one kora of 52 kilometres over the
> Dolma La at 5,630 metres. The journey every other pilgrimage is measured against.

Facts: 14 days · 5,630 m · from $2,500

Section action, ghost button: **See all yatras**

**Mechanics.** Whichever temple is nearest viewport centre becomes active: full scale, in
focus, and its significance paragraph plus fact row expand beneath it. All others blur, dim
and shrink proportionally to distance from centre, so falloff is continuous rather than a
hard toggle. Blur 0 to 4.5px, opacity 1 to 0.4, scale 1 to 0.86. Below 900px the pinning is
switched off entirely and the track becomes a native horizontal swipe with scroll-snap.
Pinned scroll-jacking on phones is reliably awful and most of this traffic is phones.

### 6.4 Which year to walk it
Day ground, asymmetric two column band. Holds the one interactive moment. **This section
was rebuilt from the research and is now the commercial centre of the homepage.**

- Eyebrow: `DEPARTURES`
- Heading: **Which year to walk it**

**Left: the Horse Year, and why 2027 is the better year**
> 2026 was the Fire Horse Year, the rarest window in the sixty year cycle, and tens of
> thousands walked the kora. The trail showed it. Permits were slow, lodges were full, and
> guides were looking after thirty people at a time.
>
> 2027 is quiet. The mountain is the same mountain. If what you came for is the circle
> rather than the crowd, this is the better year to walk it.

Action, ghost: **Join the 2027 departure list**

**Right: Nepal yatras run on your dates**
> Muktinath, Gosaikunda, Haleshi and the Shiva parikrama are arranged around you. Your
> family, your muhurta, your festival. We need three weeks' notice for permits and lodges.
>
> Janai Purnima at Gosaikunda and Shivaratri fill first, because everyone wants the same
> night.

Action, ghost: **Request your dates**

**The time-boxed band, and only if a departure can genuinely run.** Sits above both
columns until 30 September 2026, then comes down. Never faked, never left up.
> The Fire Horse Year closes on 30 September. If you can travel before then, tell us today
> and we will tell you honestly whether the permit can still be issued in time.

#### The one interactive moment: the kora ring

One per site, on theme, performed mid-journey. A brass ring the visitor holds and turns.
As it turns, an altitude readout climbs and falls across the real profile of the Kailash
kora, day markers light in sequence, and completing the circle earns the payoff.

Day markers, in order, with real altitudes:

| Marker | Place | Altitude |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Darchen | 4,675 m |
| Day 1 | Dirapuk | 4,910 m |
| Day 2 | Dolma La | 5,630 m |
| Day 2 | Zutulpuk | 4,790 m |
| Day 3 | Darchen, closed | 4,675 m |

Microcopy:
- Idle: **Hold to walk the kora**
- Mid-turn, at the pass: **Dolma La. 5,630 m. The highest point of the circle.**
- Completed: **You have closed the circle. Walked in a Horse Year it counts thirteen times.
  The last was 2026. The next is 2038.**

The payoff changed after the research. Revealing dates was a small reward for a designed
interaction. Revealing the one fact that reframes the whole decision is worth the hold, and
the visitor learns it by performing the thing it describes.

Mechanics that make it feel designed rather than gimmicky: progress builds while the
visitor holds; releasing early eases progress back down and never snaps to zero; completing
it lights the payoff and then the departure action, so the action earns something real.
Reduced motion gets the completed state with everything already shown and no hold required.

### 6.5 Check us before you pay us
Day ground. **A new section, and the highest-value thing the research produced.**

The number one objection in this category is fraud, and it is earned: an operator took over
₹15 lakh from seven pilgrims and never ran the trip, and fake credentials circulate by
email. The guidance written for buyers says an agency that is properly registered will
actively encourage you to verify it rather than asking you to take its word. Both Nepali
registers are public. So we do not display numbers, we hand over the means to check them.

- Eyebrow: `BEFORE YOU SEND MONEY`
- Heading: **Check us before you pay us**

> You are about to wire money to a company in another country, for a trip you cannot
> inspect, on the strength of a website. You should be suspicious. People in this business
> have taken deposits and disappeared.
>
> So here is everything you need to check us, and the places to check it. An operator who
> will not point you at these is not one you should pay.

| What | Number | Where to verify |
|---|---|---|
| TAAN membership | [NUMBER] | taan.org.np, searchable by name or membership number |
| Nepal Tourism Board licence | [NUMBER] | Nepal Tourism Board, Kathmandu |
| Company registration | [NUMBER] | Office of the Company Registrar |
| Parent company | Trekmania Nepal | Their public reviews, linked, clearly labelled as the parent company's |
| Our office | [ADDRESS], Kathmandu | A real address, not a contact form |

Closing line:
> If anything here does not check out, do not travel with us. Tell us what you found and we
> will fix it.

That last line is the whole section in one sentence, and no competitor will copy it.

### 6.6 How a place is confirmed
Day ground, three numbered steps.

Placed **before** the lineage section on purpose. For an international wire transfer to
Nepal, uncertainty about money is a sharper objection than uncertainty about quality.

- Eyebrow: `BOOKING`
- Heading: **How a place is confirmed**

1. **You enquire**
   > Tell us the yatra, your dates and how many are travelling. No payment, no card
   > details.
2. **We confirm and invoice**
   > We check lodges, permits and guides, then send written confirmation with a full

## 7. The vector layer plan

Everything drawn by hand. All of it honours reduced motion: final states shown, drives
stopped.

**The signature: the parikrama line.** One brass hairline, one continuous SVG path, drawn
by scroll from the first viewport to the last. It runs down the page, bends around each
section rather than through it, closes into a full circle behind the temple gallery, and
resolves into the kora ring at the Kailash moment. Driven by `stroke-dashoffset` against
scroll progress, delta-gated so converged states cost nothing.

*Loudness test: remove the line and the page becomes stacked sections instead of one walk.
It passes.*

**The brass rule.** A hairline under each section heading with a small rotated square at
its left end, echoing metal inlay on a temple door frame. Reframed here as punctuation on
the parikrama line: each rule is a segment of the same journey, and the square is a step
marker on it. One ornament, now with a reason.

**Door geometries.** Torana arch, dharma wheel, shaligram facet. Each draws its stroke on
scroll, in reading order, with a 90ms stagger between the outer and inner figure.

**The kora ring.** Brass circle, altitude arc plotted from the five real day markers, day
dots that light in sequence.

**The fixed environment layer.** One layer behind everything so scrolling feels like moving
through a place instead of past stacked sections: radial brass bloom at top centre, soft
vermilion wash upper right and brass wash lower left, `feTurbulence` paper grain at about
5% opacity, `background-attachment: fixed` so it never repaints on scroll.

**Whisper level, one per section.** Slow cycles of four seconds or longer, given negative
animation delays so they are mid-cycle at first paint, paused off-screen by an
IntersectionObserver-toggled class, and paused on hidden tabs by
`body.paused *, body.paused *::before, body.paused *::after { animation-play-state: paused !important }`
toggled on `visibilitychange`. `animation-play-state` does not inherit, which is why the
selector reaches pseudo-elements explicitly.

### 7.3 The twelve laws, applied to hero footage selection

The laws exist to predict which shots work before money moves. Here they are used as an
acceptance test on footage the operator already has. **A clip that fails laws 1, 4 or 7
cannot be rescued by engineering.**

| Law | The test on our clip |
|---|---|
| 1. Motion agrees with the scroll | Scrolling down must read as going down, opening up, or arriving. A camera rising up the mountain fights the page and always loses. Prefer a descent, an approach, or a travel along the kora. |
| 2. One subject, one continuous motion, no cuts | A single unbroken shot. Any cut in the supplied footage disqualifies it; trim to the longest clean run instead. |
| 3. Lock the path, free the body | Prayer flags, cloud, blowing dust, moving pilgrims. A frozen shot reads as dead footage. |
| 4. Plan the ending first | The last frame is where the page comes to rest. It must be a composed arrival with generous margin, because the header sits over its top and cover-crop eats its edges. Check it with the nav mocked over it, at a wide window and a short one. |
| 5. Choose forgiving subjects | Distant silhouettes, mist, snow, light and fabric render well. Close faces and hands do not. Keep pilgrims distant. |
| 6. Prefer a vertical motion axis | Where two candidate clips are equal, take the one whose motion runs with the scroll axis. |
| 7. Compose for the layout | Decide where the action sits in frame and keep that lane clear; the captions flank it. This is chosen before the band map is finalised, and the band map moves to fit the footage rather than the reverse. |
| 8. Sell the boundary crossings | Only if the clip passes through mist, a doorway, or a gate. |
| 9. Brand or frame close | Not applicable; the subject is a place, not a product. |
| 10. Text over footage earns its legibility | The four-layer system in §8, audited against the worst frame. |
| 11. Pace text in scroll distance | Bands in vh, validated by the flick test. |
| 12. The standing guards | Never build the page around footage the operator has not approved. |

**Inspection runs in both directions.** The footage sometimes improves on the plan: a
better composition, an unplanned moment. When it does, move the band map and the layout to
feature it rather than forcing the original plan. If the ending frame's subject lands
off-centre, move the settle text to honour it.

## 8. The engineering list

Named in full so the build cannot half-remember it. The standard lives in
`10k-websites/references/scrub-pipeline.md`.

**The scrub hero**
- Fetch the video as a Blob, streamed behind an honest progress ring, because many hosts
  silently lack HTTP Range support and every seek then clamps to zero on the live site
  while working perfectly locally
- Watchdog re-armed on every chunk; 20 seconds with no progress aborts into the still-image
  fallback rather than freezing the ring forever
- Poster wins the bandwidth race by design: paint it first, start the Blob fetch only once
  the poster has loaded or failed, with a 4 second safety timeout
- Lerp the displayed time in a rAF loop that rests, with `Math.pow` dt normalisation so a
  120Hz screen converges at the same speed as a 60Hz one
- IntersectionObserver so the loop never runs while the hero is scrolled past
- Gate the seeks: coalesce to the newest target, exactly one follow-up on `seeked`, and
  reset the busy flag on `error` so the gate can never deadlock
- Write to the DOM only on change; throttle any scroll-driven text to about 10Hz **and**
  only write when the string actually changed
- Promote the video to its own compositor layer so its repaints never drag the page

**Pacing and legibility**
- Bands paced in vh, not seconds. Each beat gets a fully visible plateau of roughly 80 to
  130vh with eased ramps of about 20vh at each edge
- Smoothstep opacity at both edges; band 1 skips the ease-in, band 4 skips the ease-out
- Validated by the flick test at 120, 240 and 360px wheel steps
- Four-layer legibility: global base scrim, per-band radial scrim riding `--k`, three-layer
  text shadow token, chip backings for small text. Buttons never carry the text shadow;
  a shadowed label reads as grime
- Worst-frame audit at 3.5:1 minimum, tuned against the worst frame and never the average

**The five static-hero gates**, matched character for character between the CSS media
queries and the JS decision, and kept **live** with `change` listeners so a rotating tablet
or a mid-session reduced-motion flip re-arms correctly:
```
(max-width: 720px)
(orientation: portrait) and (max-width: 1024px)
(orientation: portrait) and (pointer: coarse)
(orientation: landscape) and (pointer: coarse) and (max-height: 560px)
(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)
```

**Complete without the video.** The page must be complete and beautiful if the video never
loads. Tested on purpose by renaming the file.

**The motion system**
- Nothing snaps. Two easing tokens, used everywhere
- Entrances are choreography: `.in` added by IntersectionObserver, children arriving with
  60 to 150ms stagger. Start and end states prefixed with the container class so they win
  the cascade, and stagger delays **retired** after the entrance with a rule that matches or
  beats the specificity of the nth-child delays it retires
- Never put a dynamic style on an element that also has a `forwards` entrance animation
- Animate only `transform` and `opacity`. Glow pulses go on a pseudo-element at full
  strength with its opacity animated
- Masked text reveals get em-based descender room, or g, y and p lose their tails
- Never overwrite `el.style.transition` from JS; toggle a class declaring the full transition

**The quality floor**
- `overflow-x: clip` on both `html` and `body`, with `hidden` declared first as a fallback
- Reduced motion honoured completely and in both directions, including pseudo-elements,
  zeroed transition delays, and per-breakpoint positional transforms re-applied
- Semantic landmarks, skip link to `#main`, real heading hierarchy, `aria-hidden` on
  decoration
- The scrub video is decorative: `aria-hidden="true"`, no controls, out of the tab order
- `:focus-visible` in brass. Touch targets at least 44px under `(pointer: coarse)`, added
  without changing layout
- Contrast computed, not guessed: 4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text and interactive borders
- Text blocks sized in `ch` on the text element itself, never on a container
- Rows of text align on the baseline, not on centred boxes

### 8.1 Asset processing

No credits spent here, and the keyframe interval is the difference between smooth and
stuttering. Raw and review files live **outside** the theme, never in `assets/`.

**The scrub encode, the one that matters most.** A keyframe every 8 frames so every scroll
position seeks cleanly:
```bash
ffmpeg -i raw.mp4 -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -g 8 -keyint_min 8 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -an assets/video/hero-scrub.mp4
```
Target roughly 4 to 8 MB for a 6 second 1080p clip. Busy full-frame detail tolerates crf 25
to 26 with a downscale to about 1700px wide; smooth gradients band instead of masking, so
check the calm frames specifically and push crf gently there. Step one variable at a time,
crf first then width, and judge by scrubbing rather than by pausing.

**The ending-rest check, objective rather than a judgment call.** Difference each frame
against the last and read the motion curve; an arrival rises and falls back, a drift stays
high to the end:
```bash
ffmpeg -i raw.mp4 -vf "tblend=all_mode=difference,signalstats,metadata=print:key=lavfi.signalstats.YAVG" -f null -
```

**The tail trim**, when the shot is strong until the subject drifts near the end. Cheaper
than a re-shoot, and the page maps scroll to progress rather than to seconds, so a shorter
clip costs nothing:
```bash
ffmpeg -i raw.mp4 -t 4.3 -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -g 8 -keyint_min 8 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -an assets/video/hero-scrub.mp4
```

**Poster and ending frame.** The ending frame is a free, perfectly on-brand design asset;
reuse it in a lower section:
```bash
ffmpeg -i assets/video/hero-scrub.mp4 -frames:v 1 -q:v 2 assets/images/hero-poster.jpg
ffmpeg -sseof -0.1 -i assets/video/hero-scrub.mp4 -update 1 -frames:v 1 -q:v 2 assets/images/hero-ending.jpg
```

**Supporting stills**, one clean pass at about 1920px wide, because hosts recompress and we
want theirs to be the only lossy step:
```bash
ffmpeg -i raw-still.png -vf scale=1920:-2 -q:v 2 assets/images/section-name.jpg
```

Verify every output by extracting a frame before using it. A silent encode failure caught
now is free.

### 8.2 WordPress specifics

- Video byte size passed to JS via `wp_localize_script` so the ring has a real total when
  `Content-Length` is missing
- New style handles slot into the existing dependency-chained array in `inc/enqueue.php`,
  which already solves the Elementor cascade-order problem
- `TRIP_KAILASH_VERSION` in `functions.php` and `Version:` in `style.css` move together, or

- LiteSpeed cache purged after every asset change

### 8.3 The speed receipts

Measured on the live URL after deploy, never estimated. These are the proof the site is
fast, and they answer the standing objection to cinematic sites.

```bash
curl.exe -s -o NUL -w "TTFB %{time_starttransfer}s, total %{time_total}s, %{size_download} bytes\n" https://tripkailash.com/
```
